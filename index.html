THE BIG PICTURE

What Have We Learned?

Australia's playgrounds are more than dots on a map. Their distribution, size, facilities and relationship to surrounding open space reveal different dimensions of public recreation.

01

Location matters.

Playground density varies considerably between regions. However, the number of playgrounds per square kilometre cannot tell us how easily residents can reach them.

02

Playgrounds offer different experiences.

Not all playgrounds offer the same recorded features. Canberra's playgrounds vary in area and shade provision, while Hobart's amenity records show that seating is common but shelter is far less frequently documented.

03

Context changes the picture.

Most Manningham playgrounds fall within mapped recreational open space. Yet differences in data availability and collection methods limit what can be concluded across regions.

Understanding playground provision requires more than counting locations. Geographic context, available facilities and the quality of public data all shape what we can learn.

ABOUT THE DATA

Methodology & Limitations.

The findings in this atlas depend on how playgrounds are recorded, classified and mapped. These considerations are important when interpreting comparisons across regions.

Data coverage

This atlas combines public playground records from seven Australian study regions. These datasets differ in geographic coverage, collection dates, available attributes and definitions.

The national overview represents the study regions, not a complete inventory of every playground across Australia.

Geographic analysis

Regional playground density is calculated by dividing recorded playground counts by the corresponding administrative land area. Canberra's suburb-level density similarly accounts for suburb area.

These measures describe spatial concentration, not walking distance, population-adjusted access or the quality of playground provision.

Playground characteristics

Playground size, classification, shade and amenities are analysed only where the source datasets provide the relevant information. Similar attributes collected by different authorities may not be directly equivalent.

Hobart's amenity analysis uses 2014 records for 43 playgrounds and counts six selected facilities. These historical records do not necessarily describe present-day conditions.

Interpretation

Recorded absence of a facility should not be interpreted as a comprehensive assessment of playground quality or accessibility. Missing information is distinguished from recorded values where possible.

Manningham's open-space analysis measures whether playground locations fall within mapped recreational open-space boundaries. Locations outside those boundaries are not necessarily inaccessible to the public.
